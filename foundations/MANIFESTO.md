# The Rosetta Manifesto

**Status:** Founding Declaration (v0.1)

**Date:** 2026-07-31

---

Humanity's greatest challenge has never been a lack of intelligence.

It has been a lack of coordination.

Every generation discovers valuable knowledge — and loses too much of it.
Ideas disappear. Projects restart from scratch. Communities solve problems
others have already solved. Experts never find each other. Organizations
forget what they once knew.

We believe:

**Knowledge compounds — but only when it survives.**
Progress, science, software, and communities all compound. Rosetta optimizes
for long-term accumulation, never short-term engagement.

**Coordination is the frontier.**
The world has largely solved information distribution. The remaining challenge
is knowing who has already worked on what, what context is missing, and what
should continue from here.

**The hero is humanity.**
Rosetta is a tool — like writing, libraries, Git, Wikipedia, the Internet. Its
success is measured by how much more capable people become when using it
together.

**Stewardship endures.**
Ownership is temporary. Influence is temporary. Technology is temporary. Every
generation inherits an unfinished civilization and owes the next one a
stronger start.

**Every perspective is incomplete, and every perspective is valuable.**
The objective is not consensus. The objective is coherent understanding.

**Empower contributors; never replace them.**
AI assists humans in organizing knowledge. Human judgment stays at the center.

**No one owns the memory.**
Knowledge lives in distributed repositories anyone can hold in full, carry
away, and build upon — private by default, shared by intention, trusted
through provenance rather than position.

---

Rosetta is infrastructure for collective intelligence: a shared memory layer
for people, projects, and AI.

The distance between Earth and the stars is measured in physics.

The distance between humanity and the stars is measured in our ability to
coordinate.

Rosetta exists to help close that distance.
