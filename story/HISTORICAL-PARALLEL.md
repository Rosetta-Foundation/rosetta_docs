## Historical parallel — C2-P2 / C6-P1 (limit → tool)

**Structural claim:** When social coordination or knowledge load exceeds what
prior memory/navigation means can carry, societies develop new tools that
extend those means (share → write → organize → search → …).

**Pre-registered falsifier (before search):** Large, complex societies that
sustained coordination for generations _without_ inventing new external
memory/navigation tools — or clear cases where tools appeared without a prior
limit — would force us to treat the pattern as optional mythology, not
mechanism.

| Case                                                                           | Support / challenge                           | Marking                    | Source                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mesopotamian tokens → cuneiform under administrative load                      | Support                                       | Published source confirmed | [Schmandt-Besserat, Evolution of Writing](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/tokens/the-evolution-of-writing/); Ch. 2 research pack |
| Print abundance → indexes, catalogs, standardized citation                     | Support                                       | Published source confirmed | Essay’s own navigation history (Ch. 6); classic bibliography history (standard)                                               |
| Inca empire scale with **khipu** (not alphabetic writing) + specialist keepers | Challenge (bounds “writing” as the only tool) | Published source confirmed | [World History Encyclopedia — Quipu](https://www.worldhistory.org/Quipu/); Urton/Quilter literature cited there               |
| Library destruction / dark-age knowledge contraction                           | Challenge (tools fail; limits return)         | Published source confirmed | General historiography of Alexandria/loss episodes — use as **bound**, not single-cause myth; see adversarial note            |
| Polynesian / Māori oral-spatial mnemonic systems at voyaging scale             | Challenge (scale without writing)             | Published source confirmed | [Matheson, _Māori Orality and Extended Cognition_](https://doi.org/10.26686/wgtn.17009657)                                    |

**Assessment:** Confidence stays **Working hypothesis**, but the claim must be
stated as **functional**, not **script-chauvinist**: societies invent _some_
durable externalization or mnemonic architecture under load — writing is one
powerful family, not the only one. Khipu and oral-spatial systems are
challenging cases for “writing appeared because memory failed,” and supporting
cases for “externalization appears under load.”

**Prose implication:** Ch. 2 now includes a short clause on non-written
external records (Andean knotted-string accounts + oral keepers) and mnemonic
architectures. Catalog **C2-H0** T2 is in [`RESEARCH-GROUNDING.md`](RESEARCH-GROUNDING.md).

## Historical parallel — C7-I3 (destinations ≫ paths)

**Structural claim:** Civilizations accumulate conclusions/artifacts
(“destinations”) more readily than the reasoning routes that produced them
(“paths”).

**Pre-registered falsifier:** Durable, scalable traditions that routinely
preserve disagreement, alternatives, and rationale alongside conclusions —
widely practiced, not rare craft — would force “comparatively less skilled” to
become “unevenly skilled” or drop.

| Folders of notes without which-note-mattered (essay)                                          | Support             | W6                                              | Ch. 7                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Talmudic / layered commentary (central text + generations of argument)                        | Challenge           | Published source confirmed                      | Overview via [Talmud-IR / Talmudic discourse structure](https://www.cs.unh.edu/~dietz/papers/kusa2026talmud.pdf) (describes Mishnah/Gemara/super-commentary as sustained path-preserving discourse)                                                         |
| Architecture Decision Records (context, decision, consequences)                               | Challenge           | Published source confirmed                      | [Nygard, “Documenting Architecture Decisions” (2011)](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions); [adr.github.io](https://adr.github.io/); [Fowler, ADR](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ArchitectureDecisionRecord.html) |
| Scientific citation + methods sections (partial path)                                         | Mixed               | Published source confirmed                      | Standard scientific publishing — preserves some path, often not failed alternatives                                                                                                                                                                         |

**Assessment:** Confidence stays **Working hypothesis / interpretive**, reframed
as **comparative scarcity**, not absolute absence. Path-preserving traditions
exist and are historically powerful; they remain **labor-intensive and unevenly
adopted**, especially in ordinary professional orgs (where ADRs are recommended
precisely because the default fails).

**Prose implication:** Soften Ch. 7 absolute comparative line to acknowledge
commentary chains and decision records as real but incomplete exceptions.

## Historical parallel — C8-P2 (rediscovery under failed trails)

**Structural claim:** When an insight already exists in the common record but
trails to it are weak, later minds can re-derive the same structure without
encountering the earlier writing — and that re-derivation is evidence of a
navigation failure, not of an empty archive.

**Pre-registered falsifier (before search):** Clear cases where high-stakes
insights, once published into a shared scholarly record, are routinely found by
the relevant next workers _without_ independent re-derivation — i.e. where
publication alone reliably prevents rediscovery — would force the claim down to
“rare accident” rather than structural cost.

| Case                                                                                              | Support / challenge | Marking                    | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Mendel’s genetics unread for a generation despite publication                                     | Support             | Published source confirmed | Bush cites the episode in [_As We May Think_](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/1945/07/as-we-may-think/303881/) (1945); standard history of science (rediscovery ~1900)                                                                                                        |
| Bush (1945): record expands faster than means of consulting it; mature thought at the insert gate | Support             | Published source confirmed | Same essay — collection → parallel extraction → insertion into common record; “for mature thought there is no mechanical substitute” ([Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/1945/07/as-we-may-think/303881/); [MIT PDF](https://web.mit.edu/sts.035/www/PDFs/think.pdf)) |
| Multiple independent inventions of writing (pristine scripts)                                     | Mixed / bound       | Published source confirmed | Not “lost trail” — genuine independent invention before a shared record existed. See C2-H1 pack in [`RESEARCH-GROUNDING.md`](RESEARCH-GROUNDING.md)                                                                                                                                        |
| Modern citation + search making Mendel-class losses rarer in some fields                          | Challenge           | Published source confirmed | Directional only — improves known-item retrieval; does not erase exploratory / cross-field miss (aligns with C4-H2 / C6-H2)                                                                                                                                                                |

**Assessment:** Confidence **Working hypothesis**. Rediscovery after publication
is a documented cost of weak trails; independent invention _before_ a shared
record is a different phenomenon and must not be collapsed into the same
sentence. Bush’s essay is both a **source for Mendel** and an **instance of the
pattern** (later path-keeping architectures can reappear without a live trail
to 1945).

**Prose implication:** Ch. 8 adds one ego-free paragraph: Mendel + Bush as
structural evidence; rediscovery framed as unfindable path, never as personal
credit. Ch. 10 may add one late memoir beat (**C10-M1**) after Rosetta is named
(personal/org split noticed afterward as echoing Bush) — still no ticket IDs
in essay voice. See [`RESEARCH-GROUNDING.md`](RESEARCH-GROUNDING.md) C8 pack.
