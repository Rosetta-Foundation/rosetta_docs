## Research grounding — C2-H1 (independent invention)

**Claim (catalog):** Writing appeared independently in several parts of the world.

**Sources consulted (5):**

1. [William G. Boltz, “The Invention of Writing in China,” _Oriens Extremus_ 42 (2000/01)](http://www.oriens-extremus.de/inhalt/pdf/42/OE42-01.pdf) — **W1/W2** (peer-reviewed / domain journal). Relevance: States writing was invented _ex nihilo_ four times — Egypt, Mesopotamia, Mesoamerica, and China — and argues Chinese writing shows no credible external stimulus.
2. [Christopher Woods (ed.), _Visible Language: Inventions of Writing in the Ancient Middle East and Beyond_, Oriental Institute Museum Publications 32 (2010/2015)](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/oimp32.pdf) — **W2** (university museum scholarly catalog). Relevance: Frames four parallel pristine traditions (Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, Maya world) and compares their pathways side by side.
3. [Denise Schmandt-Besserat, “The Evolution of Writing” (UT Austin)](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/tokens/the-evolution-of-writing/) — **W2/W5** (leading specialist synthesis; treat claims as expert, not anonymous web). Relevance: Independent invention in the Near East, China, and Mesoamerica; traces Mesopotamian continuity from tokens.
4. [“History of writing,” Wikipedia (survey article; use via its cited scholarship)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing) — **W4/W5** (reference compendium). Relevance: Summarizes mainstream “four independent inventions” framing and the live Egypt↔Mesopotamia _stimulus diffusion_ debate (idea transfer possible; structural independence argued; no definitive determination).
5. [Bridget Alex, “How Humans Invented Writing — Four Different Times,” _Discover_ (2019)](https://www.discovermagazine.com/how-humans-invented-writing-four-different-times-135) — **W5** (quality long-form). Relevance: Accessible statement of the pristine-script consensus and the caveat that Egypt–Mesopotamia independence could still be revised.

**Aggregate finding:** Contemporary specialist literature **converges** on plural,
independent (“pristine”) inventions of writing — most commonly Mesopotamia,
Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica. China and Mesoamerica are especially strong
independence cases (no contact evidence with the Near East; distinct
representational methods). Egypt remains the main **live contest**: some
scholars allow stimulus diffusion of the _idea_ of writing from Mesopotamia;
others argue independent Nilotic development. False balance against wholesale
monogenesis is not required — monogenesis is a historical foil.

**Essay implication:** Keep plural independent invention; optionally name that
_at least some_ inventions were independent (already done in chapter soft).

**Confidence:** **Established by evidence** for “multiple independent
inventions”; **Contested** only on the Egypt stimulus edge case.

**What would change this:** Strong archaeological consensus that all
non-Mesopotamian systems derive by diffusion from a single origin.

## Research grounding — C2-H2 (earliest purpose practical)

**Claim (catalog):** Earliest writing was primarily practical (harvests, trade,
laws, agreements).

**Sources consulted (4):**

1. [Schmandt-Besserat, “From Accounting to Writing”](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/tokens/from-accounting-to-writing/) — **W2**. Relevance: Earliest cuneiform grows from clay-token accounting; impressed tablets continue economic record-keeping for centuries before literary/religious expansion.
2. [Schmandt-Besserat, “The Evolution of Writing”](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/tokens/the-evolution-of-writing/) — **W2**. Relevance: Pictographic stage “served exclusively for accounting” (3500–3000 BC) in Mesopotamia; earliest Chinese attestations are Shang oracle bones (divination), not administrative ledgers.
3. [Woods et al., _Visible Language_ (foreword / framing)](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/oimp32.pdf) — **W2**. Relevance: Explicitly rejects a universal “bureaucracy invented writing everywhere” story: Mesopotamian earliest tablets are economic; earliest Egyptian writing more ceremonial/display; Chinese first attested in divination; Mesoamerican hieroglyphic writing tied to religious/political contexts.
4. [Alex, _Discover_ (2019)](https://www.discovermagazine.com/how-humans-invented-writing-four-different-times-135) — **W5**. Relevance: Oldest surviving contexts differ — “economic transactions in Mesopotamia and divination rituals in China.”

**Aggregate finding:** **Partially corroborated.** The strong form (“earliest
purpose was strikingly practical: harvests, trade, laws, agreements” as a
universal) overfits Mesopotamia and compresses later legal uses into “earliest.”
The accurate claim: earliest _attested_ uses are often about durable
record-keeping under institutional pressure, but the _genre_ varies by tradition
(admin vs ritual vs display). Laws as written codes are generally later than the
invention horizon.

**Essay implication:** Soften universal “practical” list; lead with Mesopotamian
admin, note other first contexts, keep the shared need for durable records.

**Confidence:** **Working hypothesis** as civilizational generalization;
**Established by evidence** for early Mesopotamian administrative dominance.

**What would change this:** Showing that the earliest Mesopotamian corpus is
primarily non-administrative, or that all pristine traditions begin as
literary/narrative rather than record systems.

## Research grounding — C2-H3 (“for the first time” external memory)

**Claim (catalog):** Writing first allowed knowledge to exist independently of
living carriers.

**Sources consulted (3):**

1. [Schmandt-Besserat, “The Evolution of Writing”](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/tokens/the-evolution-of-writing/) — **W2**. Relevance: Token systems already “separated the data from the knower” — counters communicated to anyone initiated in the system, a change from face-to-face oral transmission. Writing then increases abstraction (2-D signs, numerals, eventually phonetics).
2. [Schmandt-Besserat, “Tokens: their Significance…”](https://sites.utexas.edu/dsb/tokens/tokens/) — **W2**. Relevance: Tokens as multi-millennial external accounting code before true writing; writing begins when 3-D tokens become 2-D impressions.
3. [“History of writing,” Wikipedia — proto-writing section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing) — **W4**. Relevance: Writing emerges from proto-writing; most proto-writing never becomes full language writing — so “first” must mean a threshold in capability, not the first external mark in human history.

**Aggregate finding:** Absolute “for the first time” is **too strong**. Durable
external marks and token accounting already externalized some knowledge.
Writing is better framed as a **qualitative threshold**: language-capable,
highly abstractable, institutionally scalable external memory — not the first
external memory _tout court_.

**Essay implication:** Replace absolute first with comparative power/scale
(“more powerfully than earlier counters and marks…”).

**Confidence:** **Working hypothesis** (threshold claim); absolute-first
formulation **Speculative** / overstated.

**What would change this:** Evidence that pre-writing external systems already
encoded open-ended linguistic content at civilizational scale comparable to
early writing — which would collapse the threshold story.
