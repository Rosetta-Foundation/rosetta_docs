# What Rosetta makes possible

**Status:** Sketch
**Altitude:** The Field Guide — *What does this let us do?*
**Audience:** builders, civic leaders, journalists, knowledge workers

This is not another README. It is a question-driven field guide to the
same ideas the [story](../story/README.md) develops and the
[conceptual model](../architecture/CONCEPTUAL-MODEL.md) makes precise.

Build from questions. Keep the jargon out. When a path is incomplete, say
so.

Nothing here claims a shipped civic product. “Could” is the honest verb
until a domain is built. Engineering examples lean on work already in
motion. Civic and personal examples show the same questions at other
scales.

---

## The questions are the product

Rosetta is useful when a person can ask a *why* that today's archives
usually cannot answer — and when the answer includes what is missing.

```text
What existed?
      ↓
What did we observe?
      ↓
What did we make of it?
      ↓
What changed because of that interpretation?
      ↓
Can someone later reconstruct that honestly?
```

If any step is gone, the honest output is not a guess. It is a map of the
gap.

---

## “Why did my property taxes go up?”

A homeowner receives a bill. The number survived. The path may not have.

```text
Your bill changed
      ↑
tax rates changed
      ↑
budgets were adopted
      ↑
proposals were debated
      ↑
staff made recommendations
      ↑
conditions and evidence produced those recommendations
```

Wayfinder could walk that chain: assessment, taxing jurisdictions, rate
changes, budget decisions, hearings, votes, and the explanations that
were actually published. Chronicle would be the place those records and
their relationships live, if a community chose to keep them.

If a hearing transcript is missing, or a recommendation has no cited
evidence, the answer should name the break. A rounded story that hides
the gap is worse than no story.

This is civic literacy, not a tax product. The same walk-back applies to
a zoning change, a school-boundary decision, or a budget cut.

## “Why does our system work this way?”

A team inherits a design whose current shape can be inspected. The
original requirements can sometimes be found. What is hardest to recover
is the sequence that made one approach seem wiser than another.

Chronicle could reconstruct:

- the decisions that produced the present architecture
- the incidents that forced a change
- the alternatives that were tried
- the approaches that were rejected, and why
- the evidence those interpretations cited

The destination is the system as it stands. The path is how it became
reasonable to the people who built it — including the disagreements they
did not clean out of the record.

## “Where did this belief come from?”

A person can hold a conclusion and lose the route.

A personal Chronicle could trace a reflection back through earlier
conversations, observations, documents, and interpretations. Not every
thought belongs there. The ones that do should remain attributable and
correctable.

The question is the same as the tax bill and the inherited system:

> What existed? What did I observe? What did I make of it? What changed
> because of that? Can I reconstruct that honestly later — or let someone
> I trust reconstruct it with me?

Personal and organizational memory are different contexts. The question
does not change. The permission to look does.

---

## “Why can't you answer?”

Because part of the path is missing.

This is becoming one of Rosetta's most distinctive ideas.

Missing is not the same as nonexistent. Unresolved is not the same as
false. A later interpretation is not the same as a contemporaneous one.
A machine reading is not the same as a human one.

Rosetta should tell you **what is missing rather than pretending to
know.**

```text
asked: Why was this decided?

found:    the decision
found:    the final document
missing:  the alternatives that were refused
missing:  the evidence the staff recommendation cited
missing:  whether this reading is contemporaneous or reconstructed
```

An instrument that fills those blanks with plausible prose has failed the
practice, even if it sounds confident.

## Same question, different scale

```text
PERSON
"What happened to me?"

        ↓

TEAM / ORGANIZATION
"Why did we do this?"

        ↓

COMMUNITY / GOVERNMENT
"Why was this decided?"

        ↓

CIVILIZATION
"How did we get here?"
```

Chronicle preserves enough of the path to investigate.
Wayfinder lets a human ask.
Rosetta is the practice and infrastructure that make the exchange
possible.
